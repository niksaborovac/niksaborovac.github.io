Internship

Fugro Survey B.V.

Nootdorp, the Netherlands

1 August - 1 October 2026

For the purpose of conducting my internship, I was a part of the Fugro Survey B.V. company located in the Netherlands.

Description of my tasks

1-7 AugustRig move

The main objective of the task was to configure the sensors and solutions to successfully position the Les Hat jack-up rig next to the ASDA platform, so the rig can extend its cantilever and the drill string can be deployed. The Navsim application was used to send simulated data. Once finished, the final fix report was generated as the last step.

10-18 AugustSurvey statistics and StarPack data visualization

Following the trainings, I was assigned to process logged GNSS data from StarPack. The reference point was included in the supplied data and based on it I derived horizontal and 3D position errors. The 2D/3D errors were visualized using the scatter plots, with the designated time interval on the x axis (14-17 August).

10-18 AugustUSBL calibration

After configuring the project and setting up the hardware, I selected Extended Cardinal Point as the calibration method. The Navsim application was used to simulate survey data. When it finished, I had to compare before/after scatters and apply the calibration values (as I was satisfied with the results). I generated the USBL cal report.

19-21 AugustQuickVision camera training

After being lectured by my mentor Sušac about the QuickVision camera, I was supporting the training of the offshore crew. My first duty was to present what kind of camera this is and what its benefits are. Later in the practical part, I had to connect devices explained during the presentation.

24 AugustGeophysical survey training

I was lectured by Edward Favell who is a principal geophysicist at Fugro. He explained what types of surveys exist (reflection and refraction), together with the instruments they use. Demir Jakupović (senior engineer) displayed the broken equipment and demonstrated what the causes of breakdowns were.

25 AugustStarPack data processing

The process began by downloading the logged data from StarPack (equipped with the Trimble card), and then extracting the GNSS records from eight .spm files utilizing the SPEXPORT software. The outcome was a single .rt27 file, which was then used as an input for the Trimble Convert to RINEX software. This RINEX file was uploaded to the CSRS-PPP online service, which derived the final PPP positions.

26-28 AugustRTK stability testing

My mentor and I tested the highest possible frequency for position outputs of the Septentrio GNSS receiver, while not overloading its CPU. The RxControl application was used to configure the receivers and adjust the frequency when needed. The position records were stable. For 100 Hz output, the CPU’s load was maxed out, and we decided to lower it to 50 Hz, which resulted in 70% CPU load.

1-2 SeptemberPHINS calibration preparation

The multiple sensors were first physically connected and later set up using their own designated software. I set up the DVL’s parameters utilizing the BBTALK software. I used PHINS web interface to define input/output ports and lever arms. The last step was to configure everything in Starfix (statuses turned green) and validate that the preparation was done correctly (heading SD was less than 0.1 degrees).

7-10 SeptemberDynamic calibration

The methodology that I followed was to calculate C-O (Computed-Observed) corrections for Octans measurements. I used the GrafNav software to derive baselines between the antennas and imported them into Excel together with their offsets from the CRP. The QC of the results and Octans observed values was done in the VBAProc software, which is part of the Starfix suite. The report was generated automatically and I submitted it as a benchmark for testing phase of the software that automates this process.

11 SeptemberUSBL calibration report QC

Emmanuel Quartey (senior processor) explained to me how to thoroughly validate USBL calibration reports and what parameters should be considered with extra care. He used several reports and compared them systematically so I would grasp it more easily.

14-25 SeptemberMBES processing

The processing component of the MBES data was mostly done in EIVA’s NaviEdit. NaviModel and NaviEdit were automatically connected and I proceeded to generate two DTMs with 0.25 and 0.1 m resolutions (combining all blocks). Out of all available options, I decided to go with the S-Scan automated cleaning tool as recommended by Mr Joshi. Afterwards I used Point Editor for the possible manual corrections, but the previous automated tool did a good job.

28 September - 1 OctoberBathymetric charts

The objective was to create three charts for a wind farm located in the North Sea near the English shore. The main functionality was to use Map Series in order to automate the process, because the original project had a demand for 95 charts to be created. Later, I edited the layout’s information using dynamic text. I did not manage to complete the keyplan overview and detail maps as my internship finished.

My overall learning impact

I grasped how to perform various technical hydrography-related tasks and systematically unify them in a single workflow.

The internship confirmed my future career goals of becoming an offshore surveyor.
